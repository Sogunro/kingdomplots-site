Family and society

Christian Singles Survey report

Christian singles told us how they date, what they look for and what money has to do with it.

By Kingdom Plots
2,381

Christian singles answered the survey.

Who took part

2,381 people answered an anonymous online survey: 1,808 women and 573 men. 1,642 live in Nigeria and 739 abroad, mostly in the USA and Canada, the UK and Ireland, and other African countries. Most are in their late twenties and almost all have never been married.

The survey asked about money, virginity and the past, looks, character, faith and church, meeting people, timing, and life after children or a first marriage. People chose to take part, mostly through social media, so the results describe them rather than every Christian single.

What we found

  • Readiness, not interest, is the bottleneck. Only 23% of women and 20% of men say they are financially ready for marriage.
  • “Ready” means different things. 86% of women say it means both earning and building together, yet 51% want a partner who earns more than them.
  • Age softens almost every rule. Virginity, children, previous marriages and looks matter less to women with age.
  • Grace for the past, firm on the future. Most women care that he lives for God now and will wait, not about his past. Men are stricter about the past, especially when young.
  • Everyone agrees on anger. A bad temper is the one near-universal dealbreaker.
  • Shyness and the wrong rooms. Half of everyone is shy, most have never used a dating app, and many churches are not helping single members meet.
  • Abroad and home need each other. Readiness is higher abroad; willingness to relocate is higher at home.

The biggest lever

86% of women say “ready” means both earning and building together. If more men believed that is enough, and more women held to it, many more marriages would happen.
